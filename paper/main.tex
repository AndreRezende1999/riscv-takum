\title{Takum Arithmetic for the RISC-V Vector Extension:\\
Encoding, Functional Simulation, and Evaluation}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Modern vector ISAs accumulate many low-precision floating-point formats
(bfloat16, FP8 variants) that inherit IEEE~754 complexities and multiply
instruction-set surface. Takum arithmetic has been proposed as a
tapered-precision alternative with a bounded dynamic range and a common
12-bit decoder. This work replicates, on the RISC-V Vector extension (RVV),
the methodology of the AVX10.2 case study: we specify a Zvtakum extension
(\texttt{vtadd.vv}, \texttt{vtsub.vv}, \texttt{vtmul.vv}, \texttt{vtdot.vv},
\texttt{vncvt.t.t}) for T8/T16/T32, implement a reference codec and ALU on
top of libtakum (both linear and logarithmic variants), build a functional
RVV simulator with takum support and instruction/CSR counters, and evaluate
three axes: representational viability on 100 SuiteSparse matrices, arithmetic
closure for T8/T16, and sparse matrix-vector multiplication. Takum T32
outperforms IEEE binary32 in the matrix-conversion benchmark, closure
percentages match the literature within sampling noise, and the proposed
instruction set replaces the 96 floating-point/conversion instructions of
RVV's current format zoo with eight takum operations.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Vector instruction sets increasingly support a zoo of low-precision formats
-- bfloat16, FP8 E4M3/E5M2, FP16 -- aiming at machine-learning and
low-precision HPC workloads \cite{ieee754,ofp8,bfloat16}. Each format adds
conversions, exception corners and decoder area; in RVV, FP8 is still absent
while bfloat16 required two dedicated extensions \cite{riscv-v-spec}.

Takum \cite{hunhold2024takum} is a tapered-precision format with a bounded
dynamic range covering the whole range at 12 bits, implying that all element
widths share the same decoder -- a property that simplifies hardware and
instruction-set design. The AVX10.2 case study \cite{hunhold2025avx102}
showed, for Intel AVX10.2, that replacing the multiple arithmetic formats by
takum (T8/T16/T32/T64) both simplifies the ISA and either matches or improves
accuracy; its conclusion explicitly suggests studying RISC-V and ARM vector
extensions.

This paper performs that study for RVV. The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a Zvtakum specification for T8/T16/T32 (Sections~\ref{sec:method}),
with provisional encodings derived from the single free OP-V \texttt{funct6}
slot;
\item a reference implementation and a functional RVV simulator with
instruction/CSR counters and automated tests (exhaustive for T8);
\item three evaluations: representational viability of 15 formats on 100
SuiteSparse matrices, arithmetic-closure reproduction of
\cite{hunhold2024takum}, and SpMV kernels in the simulator;
\item an RVV--Takum restructuring analysis mirroring the AVX10.2 tables.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Takum}
An $n$-bit takum is a string $(S, D, R, C, F)$: sign $S$, direction $D$,
three regime bits $R$, $r\in\{0,\dots,7\}$ characteristic bits $C$ and
$p = n-r-5$ fraction bits. The \emph{linear} variant (Definition~8 of
\cite{hunhold2024takum}) represents
$v = [(1-3S)+f]\,2^{e}$ with $e=(-1)^S(c+S)$; the \emph{logarithmic}
variant (its standard) represents $v = (-1)^S \sqrt{e}^{\ell}$ with
$\ell=(-1)^S(c+m)$. Bit strings shorter than 12 bits are zero-extended
(``ghost bits''), which bounds the characteristic part to 11 bits and
guarantees a common decoder. Negation is two's-complement negation of the
bit string; NaR (Not a Real) is the pattern $100\dots0$, ordered below all
values; arithmetic is saturating. For $n=8$ the effective dynamic range is
reduced ($2^{\pm239}$ linear, $e^{\pm119.5}$ logarithmic) because the
characteristic/fraction bits are partially ghosts.

\subsection{Posits and IEEE derivatives}
Posits \cite{gustafson2017posit} introduced tapered precision but suffer from
unbounded exponent codes; bfloat16 \cite{bfloat16} and OFP8 \cite{ofp8}
trade range for tiny representations. The AVX10.2 study
\cite{hunhold2025avx102} benchmarked all of these on 1401 SuiteSparse
matrices and restructured AVX10.2 into a takum-based ISA. Our work reuses the
same methodology on RVV with an open implementation: codec and ALU over
libtakum \cite{libtakum}, simulator, and benchmarks, complementing the takum
hardware codec \cite{hunhold2024vhdl} and the multi-format benchmark tool
\cite{mufolab}.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Codec and ALU}
The codec decodes fields through the 12-bit ghost expansion and computes
values independently of the arithmetic library; conversions and operations
delegate to libtakum v2.0.0 \cite{libtakum}. Two rounding modes are
implemented: round-to-nearest-even (RNE, normative) and Algorithm~1 of
\cite{hunhold2024takum} (bit-string guard rounding with saturation). On
200{,}000 random inputs per variant and width no divergence was observed,
but exact ties were not sampled. Integer comparisons follow the
two's-complement order, and NaR is detected by the \texttt{INT\_MIN}
patterns. The codec/ALU were validated exhaustively for T8 (all 256 bit
patterns; all $256^2$ operand pairs for add/sub/mul/div) and by sampled
property tests for T16/T32: monotonicity, negation, logarithmic inversion,
NaR propagation and saturation boundaries.

\subsection{Zvtakum instructions}
Table~\ref{tab:isa} lists the proposed instructions. Element width is
selected by \texttt{vtype.SEW} (8/16/32). \texttt{vtdot.vv} accumulates into
\texttt{vd[0]} with graded widening (T8$\to$T16, T16$\to$T32,
T32$\to$T64), matching the proposed \texttt{VDPP} family of
\cite{hunhold2025avx102}; the destination uses $\text{EEW}=2\cdot\text{SEW}$,
and the accumulator must be initialized by software. \texttt{vncvt.t.t}
narrows $T_{2n}\to T_n$ like \texttt{vfncvt}. Encodings use the only free
OP-V \texttt{funct6} (\texttt{0b111001}) with \texttt{funct3} as a
sub-opcode and bit~25 zero -- a provisional choice documented in the
repository. Masking follows RVV: \texttt{v0} mask, \texttt{vta}/\texttt{vma}
tail policy, no new CSRs, no FP exceptions, rounding fixed to RNE.

\subsection{RVV restructuring}
Using the machine-readable inventory of \texttt{riscv-opcodes}
(2026-09), RVV 1.0 has 375 instructions, of which 92 are floating point
(including 21 conversions and 6 FP reductions); bfloat16 adds 4 instructions
and vector crypto 41. No FP8 exists in RVV. Applying the AVX10.2 guidelines
(grouping, systematic naming B/U/S/T, format consolidation, generalization),
the 96 FP/conversion instructions collapse into eight takum operations:
the five above plus an integer conversion pair and a reserved
division/square-root pair. Comparisons become integer comparisons; bf16 and
FP16 use cases are subsumed by T16/T8.

\subsection{Functional simulator and benchmarks}
The simulator implements a RVV subset (vsetvl*, unit-stride loads/stores,
\texttt{vfadd/vfmul} in binary32, \texttt{vfredusum}, \texttt{vrgather.vv},
moves) plus Zvtakum over a configurable VLEN, with instruction, CSR-write and
active-element counters. Three benchmarks are used:
\emph{(i) representational viability}: 100 SuiteSparse matrices with
\texttt{nnz}$\le$50{,}000 are converted to 15 formats and back to a
\texttt{long double} reference, accumulating the relative 2-norm error
$\lVert r-a\rVert_2/\lVert r\rVert_2$ (the article uses all 1401 matrices
and binary128; we document the subset and use 80-bit extended precision,
which is far more accurate than any tested format);
\emph{(ii) closure}: for T8 (exhaustive) and T16 (2{,}000{,}000 sampled
pairs) within the posit range, the fraction of operations whose result is
exactly representable, following Section~5.6 of \cite{hunhold2024takum};
\emph{(iii) SpMV} $y=Ax$ with \texttt{vrgather}+\texttt{vtdot} (takum) and
\texttt{vrgather}+\texttt{vfmul}+\texttt{vfredusum} (binary32), reference in
\texttt{long double} over format-rounded inputs.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Representational viability}
Table~\ref{tab:viability} summarizes the 100 matrices (median and 90th
percentile of the relative 2-norm error; overflow counts where the format
cannot represent matrix entries). At 8 bits, IEEE FP8 overflows on 18--27\%
of matrices, while takum saturates and keeps the error bounded for 97--98\%
of matrices; at 16 bits takum beats bfloat16 and is comparable to posit16;
at 32 bits takum32 has the lowest median error (1.1e-9), better than binary32
(1.0e-8), reproducing the article's trend. The logarithmic and linear
variants are within a factor of three of each other.

\subsection{SpMV in the simulator}
Table~\ref{tab:spmv} reports the relative 2-norm error of $y$ and executed
instructions. Takum T32 is about 15$\times$ more accurate than binary32 in
the random case (2.8e-9/3.5e-9 vs.\ 5.1e-8), T16 stays near 1e-4 and T8 near
3--6e-2, tracking the representation results. The takum kernel needs
$\sim$20\% more instructions than the binary32 kernel (193 vs.\ 161 on the
random matrix) because the reduction is followed by a narrowing conversion
and a scalar store, but it replaces the whole low-precision instruction
surface; CSR traffic is one \texttt{vsetvli} per strip in both kernels.

\subsection{ISA and CSR complexity}
The current RVV format zoo (FP16 via \texttt{Zvfh}, bfloat16 via
\texttt{Zvfbfmin}/\texttt{Zvfbfwma}, binary32/64) requires 96
FP/conversion instructions and 21 conversion mnemonics. Zvtakum covers the
same low-precision ground with five implemented (eight proposed)
instructions, one rounding mode and no new CSRs; comparisons reuse integer
ordering. This mirrors the AVX10.2 result and supports the article's claim
that a single taper-precision base can replace the proliferation of derived
formats.

\subsection{Limitations}
The benchmark uses 100 matrices (not 1401) and an 80-bit reference (not
binary128); the simulator supports LMUL~1--8 but no fractional LMUL, only
binary32 among IEEE operations, and small SpMV matrices because
\texttt{vrgather} indexes are bounded by VLMAX. Encodings are provisional
(the OP-V space would have to be negotiated with the RISC-V technical
committees). The logarithmic variant follows libtakum's RNE rounding;
Algorithm~1 differed only at unsampled exact ties.

\section{Conclusion}
We replicated the AVX10.2-to-takum methodology on RISC-V's vector extension:
a Zvtakum specification for T8/T16/T32, a validated codec/ALU (both takum
variants), a functional RVV simulator with counters, and three evaluations.
Takum T32 matches or beats binary32 in representational error, closure
percentages reproduce the literature, and the proposed instruction set
shrinks RVV's low-precision FP surface from 96 to eight operations without
new CSRs or exception modes. Future work: full 1401-matrix run, hardware
codec/decoder RTL and FPGA comparison, vector \texttt{.vf} forms and
mixed-precision kernels.
